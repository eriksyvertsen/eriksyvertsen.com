title: What happened to diversification?
date: 2026-10-02

---

The most common pushback I hear re: private markets is, "I'll keep my money in the S&P. It's more diversified."

At the end of 2025, the 10 largest companies made up nearly 41% of the S&P 500. Ten years earlier, it was 19%.

Meanwhile, the companies that would have rounded out the index 20 years ago are staying private.

The US averaged 310 IPOs a year from 1980 to 2000, and 108 a year from 2001 to 2016.

![Top 10 companies' share of the S&P 500 rose from 19% in 2015 to 41% in 2025, while US IPOs per year fell from an average of 310 (1980–2000) to 108 (2001–2016).](/musings/what-happened-to-diversification.webp)

So the "safe" choice leans on ten companies more than at any point in decades, and it's missing a growing share of the economy.

Individual investors can't fix that by buying more index.
