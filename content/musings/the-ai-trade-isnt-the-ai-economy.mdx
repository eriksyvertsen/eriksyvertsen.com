Yesterday, screens went red. The Nasdaq lost about $2.3 trillion. Nvidia fell more than 9% and chips lost about 12%. By evening, the headlines had named it the worst rout since 2020.

There are two clocks in a week like this. One ticks by the second and resets every morning. The other moves slowly: one engineer, then a team, then an industry. The first clock is the AI trade. The second is the AI economy. They're easy to confuse, because the first one is louder.

The AI trade is a few companies pouring money into chips, data centers and power, and a crowd guessing whether demand will arrive in time to pay for it. Yesterday, the crowd decided the political winds had turned, and it moved all at once.

The AI economy is quieter. It's code getting written, support tickets answered, robots learning to move, drugs found a little faster. It doesn't reprice daily. It accumulates. Today coding is the richest vein. It won't stay the only one.

We like to tell the hunter's story about markets. The trader watches, waits and strikes: sells at the top, buys at the bottom, carries the kill home. It's a good story, with a hero and a moment. Most of the time, it isn't how anyone gets fed.

The numbers bear that out. From 2005 to 2024, $10,000 left alone in the S&P 500 grew to $71,750. A hunter who missed just the 10 best days, ten days out of twenty years, ended with $32,871: less than half. And the best days don't come on calm mornings. Seven of the ten arrived within two weeks of the ten worst. The feast and the famine keep the same calendar.

But there's an older story with no single moment in it. Someone carries a bag. On good days she fills it, and on bad days she fills it. Nothing heroic happens. At the end of the season, the bag is full.

I believe we're early in a long season. Not certain. Believe. Every great building time overbuilds. The canal diggers dug too many canals. The railroads laid more track than there were trains to run. Fiber went into the ground years before anyone had much to send through it. Many of the people who paid for all that lost money, and that's part of the story, not a footnote to it. Some people will lose money this time too. But the canals carried barges for a century, and the fiber is still under our feet, carrying this.

The good news and the bad news are already in the price, the way weather is already in the air. You don't argue with weather. You dress for it, and you keep walking.

So I don't try to call the turn. Nobody knows whether this was the start of a long winter or one storm in a long build. I'd rather keep filling the bag, season after season, than stand at the edge of the field waiting to strike.

One thing would change my mind, and it isn't red screens. It's a data center humming in the dark, fully powered, with nothing to do: machines built for work that never came. If capacity keeps getting cheaper and still sits idle, the season is shorter than I think. I'm watching for that. I haven't seen it. And don't think I will.

There may be more pain to come in the AI trade. I believe the AI economy underneath it is just getting started.
